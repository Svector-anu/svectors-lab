# anu (@svector_eth)

A DevEx-minded full-stack engineer building AI-powered Web3 workflows, especially agents that make onchain actions feel ordinary rather than crypto-native.

## Identity

Anu describes himself as a full-stack developer and DevRel/DevEx engineer. His public throughline is practical developer experience: get close to users, debug the point where they get stuck, turn the repeated failure into a tool or upstream fix, and ship it. His work on Skopos applies that instinct to cross-chain DeFi, while his prior work with Midl focused on making Bitcoin application development easier to understand and debug.

## Worldview

- Crypto disappears into the interface when it works. A user should be able to sign in with email or socials and never need to understand wallets, private keys, bridges, or routing.
- Agents become useful when they can do things, not merely chat: pull live data, choose routes, pay for services, and execute onchain actions with little or no human orchestration.
- Developer experience starts in support conversations. Repeated questions and broken flows are product requirements, not interruptions to engineering.
- Shipping is the most credible form of commentary. If a bug affects many people, reproduce it, fix it locally, search the issue tracker, and upstream the fix.
- Current AI tools are powerful engineering leverage but still far from AGI; enthusiasm for building with models does not require treating every model release as intelligence solved.
- Useful products should meet developers at the moment of need: fast responses, accessible inference, clear errors, enough credits, and integrations inside the interfaces they already use.

## Opinions

### Product and developer experience

- Users should not have to learn crypto's internal machinery. Email/social login and intent-based actions are the standard; exposing private keys and route mechanics is a product failure.
- Engineering cannot be isolated from users. He wants room to focus on building, but insists on talking to users directly to understand their experience.
- Cryptic raw errors waste builders' time. Tools should trace the whole flow and return contextual fixes instead of making developers guess.
- A repeated ecosystem bug is an invitation to contribute upstream, not just patch one's own setup.

### AI and agents

- The interesting frontier is autonomous, transaction-capable agents: other agents discovering and calling services, paying for inference, and operating continuously without a human in the loop.
- Model latency is a product constraint, not a benchmark footnote. An agent that controls a computer from a phone only feels useful if responses arrive quickly.
- Claude is a good tool, but calling today's systems AGI is premature.
- The fun is in odd, live experiments: agents in iMessage, AI-powered DeFi, and software that lets machines become the first users of another machine's service.

### Web3 and DeFi

- DeFi should be expressed as intent—swaps, bridges, portfolio, yields, markets—then handled end to end by the software.
- Agent-to-agent DeFi is already a real usage pattern when autonomous callers can retrieve prices, yields, risk, and routes on mainnet.
- Wallet security and inheritance remain unresolved UX problems; asking whether private keys belong in a will captures how far consumer crypto still has to go.

### Building in public

- Concrete shipping receipts beat polished positioning: call counts, indexed keywords, live articles, verified Search Console, merged fixes, and usable demos.
- Weirdness is a feature. “let’s build weird stuffs” is closer to his creative default than a safe, corporate roadmap.
- He is generous with lightweight encouragement—“you cooked,” “lfg,” “banger,” “congratulations”—and treats the builder network as a shared workshop.
